How Live Captions Work in Windows 11

Windows 11 includes a native Live Captions feature designed to automatically transcribe spoken content in real time from any audio or video playing across the operating system. By processing audio locally on your device, Live Captions functions across all applications—including web browsers, media players, video conferencing apps, and even live microphone input—without requiring an internet connection or app-specific captioning support.

System-Wide Audio Processing

Live Captions integrates directly into the Windows 11 audio subsystem. Instead of relying on individual software developers to build captioning tools into their apps, the operating system intercepts audio streams at the platform level.

  • Universal Compatibility: Any sound routed through the Windows audio engine can be transcribed, whether it comes from a YouTube video in a browser, a downloaded podcast, a local video file, or a meeting in Microsoft Teams or Zoom.
  • Microphone Transcription: In addition to transcribing incoming system audio, Live Captions can transcribe spoken input from your microphone, making it useful for in-person conversations or reviewing your own speech during calls.

On-Device Machine Learning and Privacy

The core speech-to-text processing occurs entirely on the local device using built-in speech recognition models.

  • No Cloud Dependency: Audio is processed offline. Once the initial language pack is downloaded, no speech or transcription data is sent to Microsoft cloud servers.
  • Low Latency: Local execution minimizes delay, allowing generated text to appear on-screen with near-zero lag relative to the spoken words.
  • Resource Optimization: The model is optimized for modern CPUs and Neural Processing Units (NPUs) on supported hardware, ensuring smooth transcription without heavily degrading system performance.

How to Enable and Control Live Captions

You can activate and manage Live Captions using several quick methods:

  1. Keyboard Shortcut: Press Win + Ctrl + L to instantly toggle the caption window on or off.
  2. Quick Settings: Open the Action Center from the taskbar (or press Win + A), click the Accessibility tile, and toggle Live captions.
  3. Settings App: Navigate to Settings > Accessibility > Captions to turn on the feature and configure preferences.

Placement and Customization

The Live Captions interface offers several customization options to prevent screen clutter and suit personal visibility needs:

  • Screen Positioning: The caption box can be docked at the top of the display, docked at the bottom, or set to float anywhere on the screen as a resizable window.
  • Visual Styling: Users can customize text color, font size, background color, and opacity to maximize contrast and readability.
  • Content Filtering: Built-in settings allow users to enable profanity masking to filter out sensitive or explicit language automatically.
