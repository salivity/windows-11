Windows 11 Widgets Board: Features and Content

The Windows 11 Widgets board is an integrated, customizable dashboard that delivers at-a-glance information, productivity tools, and personalized news directly to your desktop. Accessible via the taskbar or keyboard shortcuts, this flyout panel keeps users updated throughout the day without interrupting their workflow. This guide explains what the Widgets board is, how it functions, and the specific categories of content it displays.

What Is the Windows 11 Widgets Board?

The Widgets board is a dedicated overlay panel in Windows 11 powered by Microsoft services and web technologies. It is designed to act as a central hub for personal updates, task management, and web content.

You can open the board by clicking the Widgets icon on the far left or center of your taskbar, pressing the Windows Key + W shortcut, or swiping inward from the left edge of a touchscreen device.

Types of Content Displayed on the Widgets Board

The content on the Widgets board is split into two primary areas: functional app widgets and the personalized discovery feed.

1. Productivity and Microsoft 365 Widgets

These interactive cards connect directly to your Microsoft account and native apps to help manage daily tasks:

  • Calendar: Displays upcoming events, meetings, and reminders from Outlook.
  • To Do: Shows pending task lists and allows you to check off completed items.
  • OneDrive / Photos: Surfaces recent cloud storage files and photo memories.
  • Phone Link: Shows battery status, recent messages, and notifications from a paired smartphone.

2. Information and Utility Widgets

Utility widgets provide quick, real-time data updates throughout the day:

  • Weather: Current conditions, hourly forecasts, and severe weather alerts based on your location.
  • Traffic: Commute times and road conditions for saved home and work routes.
  • Watchlist (Finance): Real-time stock prices, market trends, and currency conversion rates.
  • Sports: Live scores, upcoming schedules, and standings for selected favorite teams.

3. Third-Party App Widgets

Windows 11 supports widgets created by third-party developers via the Microsoft Store. Examples include:

  • Spotify: View and control recently played music and playlists.
  • Meta Messenger: Quick access to recent conversations and online friends.
  • Xbox: Status updates, achievements, and PC Game Pass activity.
  • System Monitors: Hardware monitoring widgets (such as CPU and GPU usage tools) created through developer packages like Microsoft Dev Home.

4. Personalized News and Media Feed

Below the app widgets, the board hosts a continuous content feed powered by Microsoft Start (formerly MSN). This feed aggregates content based on your browsing habits and preferences, including:

  • Top Headlines: Breaking national and international news from major publishers.
  • Entertainment and Lifestyle: Trending stories, pop culture, recipes, and wellness articles.
  • Custom Topics: User-selected categories such as technology, science, gaming, or automotive.

Customization and Controls

Users have full control over the layout and content of the Widgets board. Individual widgets can be resized (Small, Medium, Large), pinned to the top of the board, customized, or removed entirely. Users can also fine-tune the news feed by liking, hiding, or following specific publishers and topics, or even disable the news feed entirely to keep the board focused solely on productivity widgets.
