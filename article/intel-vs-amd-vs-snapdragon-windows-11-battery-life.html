Intel vs AMD vs Snapdragon: Windows 11 Battery Life

Windows 11 laptops have entered a new era of power efficiency, driven by competition among Qualcomm’s ARM-based Snapdragon X series, Intel’s Core Ultra (Series 2 "Lunar Lake"), and AMD’s Ryzen AI 300 ("Strix Point") processors. This article compares how these three architectures perform in real-world battery life tests, examining how each platform handles light productivity, multimedia playback, and demanding workloads.

The Contenders and Their Architectures

  • Qualcomm Snapdragon X (Elite / Plus): Built on ARM architecture using TSMC's 4nm process. It relies on high-efficiency custom Oryon CPU cores to deliver low idle power draw and extended battery life for native Windows on ARM applications.
  • Intel Core Ultra Series 2 (Lunar Lake): An x86 platform redesigned entirely for efficiency. It features on-package Memory (MoP), redesigned Lion Cove P-cores, and Skymont low-power E-cores with no Hyper-Threading, manufactured on TSMC's N3B node.
  • AMD Ryzen AI 300 Series (Strix Point): An x86 architecture utilizing a hybrid mix of Zen 5 and compact Zen 5c cores built on TSMC's 4nm process, targeting balanced multi-threaded performance alongside sustained efficiency.

Battery Life by Workload

1. Idle and Light Productivity (Web Browsing, Office Apps)

  • Snapdragon X: Excels significantly during pure idle states and light everyday tasks. In continuous web browsing tests over Wi-Fi, Snapdragon X devices regularly achieve between 14 to 18 hours of runtime on standard 55–70 Wh batteries.
  • Intel Core Ultra (Lunar Lake): Narrowed the gap with ARM. Thanks to aggressive power-gating and efficient low-power island E-cores, Core Ultra Series 2 laptops often match or slightly exceed Snapdragon X in web browsing, delivering 15 to 19 hours under similar conditions.
  • AMD Ryzen AI 300: Shows solid improvements over older generations, averaging 11 to 14 hours in basic web and office tasks. While competitive, its idle power consumption remains slightly higher than Intel Lunar Lake and Qualcomm.

2. Video Playback (Local and Streaming)

  • Intel Core Ultra (Series 2): Takes the lead in continuous local and streaming video playback (1080p/4K) due to dedicated, low-power media engines. It frequently reaches 18 to 22+ hours on a single charge.
  • Snapdragon X: Follows closely behind Intel, providing around 16 to 20 hours of continuous local video playback.
  • AMD Ryzen AI 300: Delivers reliable video playback times of roughly 13 to 16 hours, trailing both Intel and Qualcomm in dedicated media playback efficiency.

3. Sustained Workloads and Multitasking

  • AMD Ryzen AI 300: Delivers the highest raw multi-core compute among the three while preserving reasonable battery performance (6 to 9 hours under continuous heavy loads).
  • Intel Core Ultra (Series 2): Offers roughly 7 to 10 hours under moderate-to-heavy mixed loads, prioritizing efficiency per watt over maximum multi-core throughput.
  • Snapdragon X: Highly efficient when running native ARM applications. However, running heavy x86 apps through Windows 11’s Prism emulation layer increases power consumption, dropping battery life to around 6 to 8 hours in non-native scenarios.

Which Platform Offers the Best Battery Life?

  • For Maximum Overall Endurance: Intel Core Ultra (Series 2) currently holds a slight edge in mixed media, streaming, and daily web productivity without software compatibility compromises.
  • For Native Daily Tasks and Idle Efficiency: Qualcomm Snapdragon X provides market-leading standby times and long endurance, provided workflows rely primarily on ARM-native applications.
  • For Performance-Oriented Workflows: AMD Ryzen AI 300 offers the best balance of heavy multi-core processing power with respectable all-day battery life.
