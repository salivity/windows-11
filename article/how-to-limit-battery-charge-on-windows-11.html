How to Limit Battery Charge on Windows 11

Setting a battery charge threshold caps your laptop's maximum charge level—usually at 80%—to significantly extend the overall lifespan of lithium-ion batteries. While Windows 11 does not feature a single, universal switch to limit charging, you can easily enable this threshold using OEM manufacturer software, BIOS/UEFI settings, or the Windows 11 Smart Charging feature supported by select hardware.

Method 1: Use OEM Manufacturer Software

Most laptop brands provide proprietary control software preinstalled on Windows 11 to manage battery thresholds.

  • Lenovo: Open the Lenovo Vantage or Lenovo Commercial Vantage app. Navigate to Device > Power, locate Battery Charge Threshold or Conservation Mode, and toggle it on to cap the charge at 75–80%.
  • ASUS: Open the MyASUS app. Go to Customization (or Hardware Settings) > Power & Performance. Under Battery Health Charging, select Balanced Mode (80% limit) or Maximum Lifespan Mode (60% limit).
  • Dell: Open Dell Optimizer or Dell Power Manager. Go to the Battery section, click the settings icon, and select Primarily AC Use or choose Custom to define your desired start and stop charging percentages.
  • MSI: Open the MSI Center (or Dragon Center). Navigate to Features > System Diagnosis (or Battery Master) and select Balanced (stops at 80%) or Best for Battery (stops at 60%).
  • LG: Open LG Smart Assistant (or LG Control Center), select Power Management, and turn on Extend Battery Life to limit the charge to 80%.

Method 2: Configure the Limit in BIOS/UEFI

For devices without dedicated desktop software (such as many HP business laptops), battery thresholds can be enabled directly at the firmware level.

  1. Restart your laptop.
  2. Press the manufacturer-specific key during startup (typically F10 for HP, F2 or Del for ASUS/Dell/Lenovo) to enter the BIOS/UEFI menu.
  3. Look for a section named Power, Advanced, or System Configuration.
  4. Find settings such as Battery Care Function, Adaptive Battery Optimizer, or Peak Shift.
  5. Set your desired maximum charge limit (e.g., 80%), save the changes, and exit BIOS to boot into Windows 11.

Method 3: Use Microsoft Surface Smart Charging

If you are using a Microsoft Surface device:

  1. Open the Surface app on Windows 11.
  2. Expand the Smart Charging section.
  3. Set the toggle to On to automatically limit the charge to 80% whenever the device is plugged in for extended periods.
