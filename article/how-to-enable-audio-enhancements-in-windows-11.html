How to Enable Audio Enhancements in Windows 11

Windows 11 includes built-in audio enhancement features that allow you to significantly improve your listening experience. This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on how to access and enable settings such as Bass Boost, Loudness Equalization, and Virtual Surround Sound using both the modern Windows 11 Settings app and the legacy Sound Control Panel.

Method 1: Using Windows 11 Settings

  1. Press Windows Key + I to open the Settings app.
  2. Navigate to System on the left menu, then click Sound.
  3. Under the Output section, click on your active playback device (such as your Speakers or Headphones).
  4. Scroll down to the Output settings section.
  5. Locate the Audio enhancements option.
  6. Click the dropdown menu next to it and select Device Default Effects (or toggle it On depending on your driver version).

Method 2: Using the Classic Sound Control Panel (Recommended for Specific Effects)

To access specific granular effects like Bass Boost and Loudness Equalization, use the legacy Control Panel:

  1. Press Windows Key + R to open the Run dialog box.
  2. Type mmsys.cpl and press Enter to open the Sound Control Panel directly.
  3. In the Playback tab, select your default audio device and click Properties (or double-click the device).
  4. Navigate to the Enhancements tab at the top of the properties window.
  5. Check the boxes for the features you want to activate:
    • Bass Boost: Increases the lowest audible frequencies. Select it and click Settings to adjust the boost level and cutoff frequency.
    • Loudness Equalization: Balances volume levels across different types of content so quiet sounds are louder and loud sounds are reduced.
    • Virtual Surround: Simulates a multi-channel surround sound setup on stereo speakers or headphones.
    • Room Correction: Optimizes sound based on room acoustics (requires a calibration microphone).
  6. Click Apply, then click OK to save your changes.

What to Do If the Enhancements Tab Is Missing

If the Enhancements tab does not appear in your device properties, your audio driver may not support standard Windows enhancements, or it may use proprietary software instead:

  1. Check Manufacturer Software: Look for dedicated control apps installed on your PC, such as Realtek Audio Console, Waves MaxxAudio, Nahimic, or Dolby Access.
  2. Switch to the Generic Microsoft Driver:
    • Right-click the Start button and select Device Manager.
    • Expand Sound, video and game controllers.
    • Right-click your audio device (e.g., Realtek High Definition Audio) and select Update driver.
    • Choose Browse my computer for drivers, then Let me pick from a list of available drivers on my computer.
    • Select High Definition Audio Device and click Next.
    • Restart your computer and check the Sound Control Panel again.
