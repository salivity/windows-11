Configure Windows 11 Spatial Audio for Headsets

Spatial audio in Windows 11 enhances your gaming experience by creating a 360-degree, three-dimensional soundscape that allows you to pinpoint directional cues like footsteps and gunfire. This guide explains how to enable and configure built-in and third-party spatial sound formats—including Windows Sonic, Dolby Atmos for Headphones, and DTS Headphone:X—specifically for compatible gaming headsets, ensuring optimal in-game performance.

Step 1: Connect and Select Your Headset

  1. Plug your gaming headset into your PC via 3.5mm jack, USB, or wireless dongle/Bluetooth.
  2. Press Windows Key + I to open the Settings app.
  3. Navigate to System > Sound.
  4. Under the Output section, choose your connected gaming headset as the default playback device.

Step 2: Enable Spatial Sound in Windows Settings

  1. In the Sound settings menu, click the arrow (>) next to your gaming headset to open its Properties.
  2. Scroll down to the Spatial audio section.
  3. Open the Type dropdown menu and select your preferred spatial sound format:
    • Windows Sonic for Headphones: Built into Windows 11 for free and compatible with any standard stereo headset.
    • Dolby Atmos for Headphones: Requires the Dolby Access app from the Microsoft Store (paid license with a free trial).
    • DTS Headphone:X: Requires the DTS Sound Unbound app from the Microsoft Store (paid license with a free trial).
  4. Once selected, Windows will instantly apply the spatial sound processing to your headset.

Step 3: Configure Advanced Audio Properties

To ensure the highest sound quality without processing bottlenecks:

  1. In the headset's Properties page, scroll to Audio enhancements.
  2. Set the dropdown to Device Default Effects or Off if your spatial audio app manages EQ directly.
  3. Under Format, ensure the sample rate is set to at least 24-bit, 48000 Hz (Studio Quality) for standard gaming compatibility.

Step 4: Prevent Software Conflicts

Many gaming headsets from brands like Logitech, Razer, SteelSeries, or Corsair come with proprietary software that includes built-in virtual 7.1 surround sound.

  • Disable proprietary virtual surround: Turn off 7.1 virtual surround in your headset’s manufacturer software (such as Razer Synapse, Logitech G HUB, or SteelSeries GG) before enabling Windows 11 spatial audio. Running two spatialization engines simultaneously will cause distorted or muffled sound.
  • In-Game Settings: When playing games with native spatial audio support (such as Cyberpunk 2077, Call of Duty, or Overwatch 2), select "Spatial Audio," "Dolby Atmos," or "3D Headphones" within the in-game sound settings to allow direct channel routing.
