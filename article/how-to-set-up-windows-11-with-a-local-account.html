How to Set Up Windows 11 with a Local Account

Setting up Windows 11 typically requires signing in with an active Microsoft account, but you can bypass this requirement to create a traditional, offline local user account instead. This guide provides the exact steps needed to bypass the mandatory internet connection and account screen during the Windows 11 initial setup process, allowing you to use your computer with enhanced privacy and full offline functionality.

Method 1: The Bypass Command (Recommended)

This method works during the Out-of-Box Experience (OOBE) setup screen on both Windows 11 Home and Pro editions.

  1. Start the Setup Process: Turn on your PC, select your region, and choose your keyboard layout.
  2. Open the Command Prompt: When you reach the "Let's connect you to a network" or "Sign in with Microsoft" screen, press Shift + F10 on your keyboard (or Shift + Fn + F10 on some laptops).
  3. Execute the Bypass Command: In the black Command Prompt window, type the following command exactly as shown and press Enter: 
    oobe\bypassnro
  4. Wait for Restart: Your computer will automatically reboot and return to the initial setup screen.
  5. Proceed Without Internet: Go through the region and keyboard selection again. When you reach the network connection screen, a new option will appear. Click "I don't have internet" in the bottom-left corner.
  6. Continue Limited Setup: On the next screen, click "Continue with limited setup".
  7. Create Your Local Account: Enter your desired username, click Next, set an optional password, and complete the remaining standard privacy settings to reach your desktop.

Method 2: The Blocked Email Method

If the command prompt method is unavailable, you can force Windows to offer a local account by entering an overloaded or invalid email address.

  1. Reach the Sign-In Screen: Connect to the internet and proceed until you reach the "Let's add your Microsoft account" screen.
  2. Enter a Locked Email: In the email field, enter a generic, frequently blocked address such as no@thankyou.com or a@a.com, then click Next.
  3. Enter Any Password: Type any random characters into the password field and click Sign in.
  4. Bypass the Error: Windows will display an error message stating, "Oops, something went wrong." Click Next.
  5. Create Local Credentials: Because the Microsoft account sign-in failed, Windows will automatically default to the local account creation screen. Enter your preferred local username and password to finish setup.
