How to Create and Manage Storage Spaces in Windows 11

Storage Spaces in Windows 11 allows you to combine multiple physical drives into a single virtual storage pool to protect your data from drive failures or expand your overall storage capacity. This guide provides a direct, step-by-step walkthrough on how to prepare your drives, set up a new storage pool and virtual space, select the right resiliency type, and manage your configuration through Windows 11 Settings.

Requirements Before Starting

  • Drives: At least two physical drives (internal or external via USB/SATA) to create a redundant pool. A single drive can be used for a basic pool with no data protection.
  • Data Backup: Ensure all drives you intend to add are backed up, as creating a pool will erase all existing data on them.
  • Permissions: Administrator access on your Windows 11 PC.

How to Create a Storage Pool and Space

Windows 11 includes a dedicated Storage Spaces interface within the modern Settings app.

  1. Open Settings (press Windows Key + I).
  2. Navigate to System > Storage.
  3. Scroll down and click Advanced storage settings, then select Storage Spaces.
  4. Click Create a storage pool and space.
  5. Enter a Name for your storage pool.
  6. Select the physical drives you want to include in the pool and click Create.
  7. Configure your storage space:
    • Name: Enter a label for the virtual drive.
    • Size: Allocate the desired capacity (you can overprovision beyond your physical drive limits if needed).
    • Resiliency: Choose your desired data protection level (see descriptions below).
  8. Click Create.
  9. Set the drive letter, choose the file system (NTFS or ReFS), and assign a volume label.
  10. Click Format to finish.

Understanding Resiliency Types

When setting up your storage space, select the option that best matches your performance and redundancy needs:

  • Simple (No resiliency): Stripes data across drives. Increases performance and maximizes capacity but provides no protection against drive failure. Requires at least 1 drive.
  • Two-way mirror: Writes two copies of your data across separate drives. Protects against a single drive failure. Requires at least 2 drives.
  • Three-way mirror: Writes three copies of your data across separate drives. Protects against the simultaneous failure of two drives. Requires at least 5 drives.
  • Parity: Stores data alongside parity information to protect against a single drive failure while maximizing usable space. Best for large archives and sequential read/write operations. Requires at least 3 drives.

How to Manage Existing Storage Spaces

You can manage, expand, or repair your storage pools at any time via Settings > System > Storage > Advanced storage settings > Storage Spaces.

Adding Drives to an Existing Pool

  1. Select your storage pool from the list.
  2. Click Physical disks, then select Add disks.
  3. Choose the new drive(s) and confirm.

Expanding a Storage Space

  1. Expand the storage pool containing the space you want to resize.
  2. Click the Properties button next to the specific space.
  3. Under the Size section, enter the new, larger capacity and confirm the change.

Removing or Replacing a Drive

  1. Expand the pool and locate the Physical disks section.
  2. Find the drive you want to remove and click Properties.
  3. Click Prepare for removal and leave your PC powered on until the status changes to "Ready to remove."
  4. Click Remove to safely disconnect the drive from the pool.

Deleting a Space or Pool

  1. Go to the space you wish to delete, click Properties, and select Delete space (this erases all data on that space).
  2. Once all spaces within a pool are deleted, click Delete pool to release the physical disks back into general use.
